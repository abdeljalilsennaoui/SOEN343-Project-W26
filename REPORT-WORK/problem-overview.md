# Problem Overview

With growing urban populations, cities are facing increasing challenges managing transportation efficiently. In the past two decades, a wide range of transportation services have emerged, including bike-sharing systems, car-sharing systems, public transportation platforms, e-scooters, and many online resources for available parking. While all of these services combined provide flexibility and many options for citizens to travel from point a to b in the city, they operate in isolation, each having their own running system, application that must be downloaded, and payment system.

The separation of applications causes a huge inefficiency for users. Citizens who utilize different methods must switch between multiple different platforms. This results in increased planning times, as the lack of integration between services prevents users from making fast real-time decisions between different modes of transportation.

Another major issue is limited availability of real-time data for all the services. Current systems do not provide combined information of transportation options, including vehicle availability, transit schedules, and parking availability in one single interface. As a result, users will face delays, reducing the overall efficiency of urban mobility. Additionally, since users must manage multiple different applications which means different accounts and payment methods, this will further discourage users from wanting to adopt these different platforms.

From another perspective, city administrators also face challenges from segregated mobility applications. Without centralized mobility data, it becomes extremely difficult to conclude on service improvements that need to be implemented.

Ultimately, the absence of a unified mobility platform limits the accessibility and efficiency of urban transportation. It discourages the use of environmentally friendly options to commute  such as public transit, bikes, scooters, and shared mobility. Addressing this gap is essential for promoting more sustainable urban mobility systems.


SUMMARY
- cities are facing more transportation challenges as population grows
- many services exist (bike sharing, car sharing, public transit, scooters, parking) but all run separately
- each service has its own app, system, and payment method
- users must switch between multiple apps → inefficient and time consuming
- lack of integration makes it hard to make quick real-time travel decisions
- no single platform shows all real-time data (vehicles, transit, parking)
- this leads to delays and less efficient commuting
- managing multiple accounts and payments discourages users from using these services
- city administrators lack centralized data → harder to improve services
